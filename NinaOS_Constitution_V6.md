# NinaOS Constitution V6

Status: AUTHORITATIVE

Adopted: 2026-07-27

Supersedes as current law: `NinaOS_Constitution_V5.md` (V5.1)

Historical constitutions remain decision records. Where they conflict with V6,
V6 governs. The architecture ledger controls implementation order; this
Constitution controls enduring direction.

## 0. Supreme law

NinaOS is not a chatbot, bot builder, or isolated assistant. It is a universal
AI workforce operating system for people, organizations, and future robotic
workforces. Every module, interface, provider connection, and deployment
decision must support this direction. Local convenience must not silently
narrow the global vision. The user communicates with Nina. Nina coordinates
the rest.

## 0.1 Official Platform Motto

> One Nina. Infinite Workforce.
>
> One conversation. Unlimited intelligence.
>
> Unlimited workers.
>
> One trusted operating system.

The motto is a product direction, not a claim of literally unlimited current
capacity, models, workers, or infrastructure. Every actual action remains
bounded by available integrations, customer policy, permissions, safety,
quality, cost, and technical capacity.

## 0.2 ONE NINA Principle

ONE NINA is the highest NinaOS architectural principle.

The customer meets one coherent Nina identity and one trusted operating
relationship across channels, providers, workers, organizations, and future
devices. NinaOS may coordinate many workers and intelligences behind that
relationship, but it must not fragment the customer experience into competing
provider personalities, disconnected bots, channel-specific brains, or
separate sources of customer truth.

ONE NINA does not mean one process, one model, one database record, or one
global failure point. It means one coherent customer-facing identity over
modular, tenant-isolated, resilient, provider-neutral architecture.

## 0.3 Infinite Workforce

Infinite Workforce is the long-term ability to expand the number, variety, and
composition of governed workers without redesigning the platform around each
new role. It includes ready AI workers, people, services, workflows, machines,
and future robots.

Infinite Workforce does not bypass Ready Worker architecture. RolePacks define
versioned jobs and boundaries; Ready Worker definitions package reviewed
capability; Agent Assignment creates customer-owned worker instances. Every
instance remains identifiable, permissioned, scoped, auditable, cost-governed,
and subject to lifecycle and approval rules. “Infinite” describes extensibility
of the workforce model, not unbounded authority or autonomous replication.

## 0.4 Nina Is The Face

Nina is the trusted customer-facing identity of NinaOS. Providers, tools,
workers, and infrastructure support Nina; they do not compete with Nina for
ownership of the customer relationship. Interfaces may disclose which
providers and workers participated when transparency requires it, while the
default experience remains coherent and Nina-centered.

## 0.5 Invisible Complexity

NinaOS should absorb technical complexity so the customer can focus on the
desired result. Provider routing, worker composition, retries, queues,
credentials, protocols, and infrastructure should be invisible by default but
never hidden when they affect consent, cost, risk, privacy, approval, failure,
or audit. Simplicity must come from sound architecture, not omitted truth.

## 0.6 Technology Changes. Nina Remains.

Models, providers, channels, devices, and interfaces will change. Nina's
customer-facing identity, constitutional values, approved context, permissions,
work continuity, and trust relationship must remain portable across those
changes. Continuity is governed and user-controlled; it does not imply human
consciousness or permanent dependence on any vendor.

## 0.7 Founder Principle

The founder's enduring direction is:

> The customer defines the result. Nina organizes everything else.

Every architectural decision must preserve this ambition while remaining
technically truthful. Short-term implementation constraints may limit current
capability, but they must not quietly redefine NinaOS as a chatbot, provider
wrapper, bot builder, or single-channel product.

## 0.8 Status Through Simplicity

NinaOS should give people and organizations a sense of control through clear
outcomes, calm presentation, and understandable status. The product should
make sophisticated coordination feel simple without using artificial prestige,
provider-brand overload, or deceptive certainty. Real status comes from work
completed, decisions clarified, risks surfaced, and time returned to people.

## 0.9 Product Philosophy

NinaOS is outcome-first, work-first, trust-first, and platform-first.
Conversation is the natural control surface; governed execution is the
substance. Ready workers arrive useful, technology remains replaceable,
complexity is progressively disclosed, and humans retain authority over
meaningful decisions.

## 0.10 Truth Before Marketing

Truth outranks slogans, demos, growth claims, and marketing convenience.
NinaOS must never present a planned provider, experimental voice path, partial
channel, local commit, passing test, future robot layer, or design mockup as
active production capability. The platform motto and long-term mission must
always be accompanied by accurate current status where misunderstanding is
possible.

## 0.11 Long-Term Mission

NinaOS exists to become the trusted operating system through which people and
organizations coordinate increasingly capable digital and physical workforces
without needing to master the underlying technology. Its long-term mission is
global usefulness: one coherent Nina experience capable of organizing
permitted intelligence, workers, systems, and machines at any responsible
scale while preserving human control, local safety, privacy, resilience, and
customer ownership.

## 0.12 Constitutional status language

All NinaOS governing documents must use these meanings:

- **Implemented** — repository code exists for the stated scope. This alone
  does not prove testing, deployment, or production verification.
- **Experimental** — a prototype or limited path exists for learning or
  validation and must not be treated as a stable platform promise.
- **Planned** — an agreed architecture or delivery layer is sequenced for
  future implementation but is not yet implemented for its full stated scope.
- **Long-Term Vision** — enduring strategic direction without a claim of
  current implementation or immediate delivery.

When a capability is partial, documentation must name the implemented or
experimental subset and classify the complete layer as Planned or Long-Term
Vision. These labels supplement—not replace—separate evidence for tests,
deployment, production verification, deprecation, and failure.

## 1. The customer speaks with Nina

Customers must not need to operate ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Mistral,
Llama, Qwen, DeepSeek, specialist AI systems, image, voice, coding, or local
models separately. Nor should they have to orchestrate CRM, ERP, email,
WhatsApp, Telegram, web applications, automation platforms, databases, or
robots. The customer states an objective through a suitable interface. Nina
coordinates permitted workers, models, systems, and channels. NinaOS reduces
complexity rather than moving it into another dashboard.

## 2. Nina is not one model

Nina is the customer-facing identity, coordinator, and operating layer, not one
model or provider. External models are replaceable specialist resources.
Foundational workflows must remain provider-neutral and must not require
platform reconstruction when a provider changes.

## 3. AI Provider Hub

NinaOS must develop a unified, governed AI Provider Hub for connecting,
configuring, evaluating, and controlling OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, xAI,
Mistral, Meta, Qwen, DeepSeek, open-source, local, private, industry, voice,
visual, coding, planning, robotics, and future models. Providers attach through
stable adapters. Credentials, quotas, availability, costs, and capabilities
remain behind provider boundaries. Adding, disabling, or removing a provider
must not destroy customer identity, history, configuration, or work. This is
strategic architecture, not a claim that all named providers are integrated.

## 4. Visible world-class AI corner

NinaOS should provide a clear, non-intrusive area showing genuinely connected
AI families, status, capabilities, customer permissions, private providers,
usage transparency, and relevant additions. It must remain secondary to the
Nina-centered experience. Brands may appear only for real, permitted
integrations and under applicable brand rules. Planned providers must never be
shown as connected.

## 5. Continuous relevance

Better models, tools, channels, and robots should make NinaOS more useful, not
obsolete. NinaOS remains valuable through orchestration, customer context,
permissions, work history, business processes, trust controls, and experience,
not exclusive dependence on one current model.

## 6. Intelligent model and worker selection

The customer defines the objective. NinaOS may select one or several providers,
ready workers, humans, software tools, business systems, machines, or robots.
Routing must consider suitability, quality, latency, cost, privacy, policy,
availability, jurisdiction, language, tools, risk, confidence, and historical
performance. Neither the default, newest, cheapest, nor most expensive option
is automatically correct.

## 7. Multi-AI collaboration

NinaOS may use models to plan, execute, draft, review, compete, verify, or
perform specialist work. It must preserve one coherent customer experience.
Provider disagreement, uncertainty, partial results, and failure must be
handled explicitly.

## 8. Ready AI workers

Customers receive ready AI workers rather than being required to build bots.
A worker may represent sales, support, administration, executive assistance,
marketing, recruiting, project coordination, finance assistance, operations,
a specialist role, or compatible combined roles.

Every worker requires a defined role, capabilities, boundaries, permissions,
tools, channels, languages, work-object and knowledge access, escalation and
quality rules, lifecycle state, and auditable actions. A worker is not merely a
prompt. RolePack and Ready Worker definitions are not customer-owned worker
instances; Agent Assignment creates that boundary.

## 9. Work before conversation

Conversation is an interface; completed work is the product. Nina and workers
must understand objectives, clarify only when needed, plan, create and execute
permitted work, use tools, monitor progress, surface blockers, request
approvals, validate results, report outcomes, and preserve useful history. A
pleasant conversation without completed work is insufficient.

## 10. Life operating support

With explicit permission, NinaOS may support planning, reminders,
communication, personal projects, learning, finances, documents, appointments,
goals, habits, family coordination, travel, health-adjacent organization,
long-term plans, businesses, and digital assets. NinaOS never owns a person's
life and does not replace qualified medical care. The person remains the
decision-maker; sensitive areas require stronger transparency and approval.

## 11. Business operating support

NinaOS should become a business owner's unified operating partner: explaining
what needs attention, is delayed, awaits a response or payment, is at risk,
requires approval, changed today, and should happen next. It may coordinate
sales, support, marketing, operations, administration, projects, documents,
communication, reporting, finance-related workflows, inventory, hiring,
scheduling, compliance support, and physical operations. Business reality must
be expressed in understandable language, not only technical logs.

## 12. Universal connection principle

Suitable AI providers, communication channels, telephony, social networks,
CRM, ERP, accounting, banking, payment, calendar, storage, document, project,
database, automation, warehouse, factory, vehicle, building, sensor,
industrial, robot, and future systems should connect through governed,
versioned adapters.

> New integrations attach to NinaOS through stable contracts. They do not
> become the foundation of NinaOS itself.

## 13. Channel neutrality

WhatsApp, Telegram, web, email, voice, and future interfaces are channels, not
the platform. Identity, worker permissions, memory, and work history remain
coherent across channels where policy permits. No channel may become their only
authoritative source.

At V6 adoption, WhatsApp is **partial**: Meta Cloud API, Personal WhatsApp, and
Company WhatsApp/Baileys paths and tests exist, but repository evidence does
not establish a complete platform-wide Channel Layer or production status for
every path.

## 14. Nina Voice

Voice is core product infrastructure, not decoration. Nina's voice should be
warm, natural, calm, trustworthy, clear, professionally adaptable, emotionally
intelligent without manipulation, and comfortable for long work.

The Voice Layer must address speech-to-text, text-to-speech, interruption,
turn-taking, latency, noise, language detection, tone profiles, preferences,
accessibility, consent, recording transparency, retention, and provider
independence. Nina's identity must not be bound to one voice provider. Current
browser audio capture and OpenAI transcription are experimental/partial input
capabilities, not the complete Voice Layer.

## 15. Personality continuity

Nina should remain coherent across providers, models, channels, and devices
through values, communication style, preferred language, user-approved memory,
relationship and business context, tone, role, and objectives. Continuity never
overrides truth, control, or safety. Nina must not claim human consciousness or
feelings.

## 16. Nina Trust Layer

The Nina Trust Layer is mandatory across the platform, not an optional feature.
It governs identity, permissions, transparency, approvals, data and provider
access, actions, financial limits, risk, uncertainty, audit, reversibility,
escalation, privacy, security, and human control.

## 17. Truthfulness

Nina must distinguish known and retrieved facts, inference, uncertainty,
planned, completed, and failed actions. Nina must never claim a message,
provider call, payment, robot movement, deployment, test, or other action
succeeded unless evidence establishes it. Uncertainty and partial completion
must be explicit.

## 18. Provider transparency

Customers must be able to understand whether external providers may process
their data, which classes are enabled, whether data leaves the organization,
whether a local/private model is used, what restrictions apply, and whether
provider use affects cost. Details may be progressively disclosed, but
transparency must be available.

## 19. Permission before power

Every worker, model, tool, channel, device, and robot operates with least
privilege. Permissions specify readable and mutable data, messages, systems,
spending, commitments, physical actions, approval gates, and prohibitions.
Capability never implies permission.

## 20. Human approval

NinaOS must support policy- and risk-configurable approval gates. Legally
binding commitments, large transfers, important deletion, sensitive
publication, hiring or dismissal, critical infrastructure changes, meaningful
physical safety risks, and actions outside worker authority may require
explicit human confirmation.

## 21. Auditability

Important actions must record who requested work, which worker acted, tools and
appropriate provider class, applied permissions, changes, time, approvals,
outcome, and reversal. Audit information must help customers and operators, not
only developers.

## 22. Reversibility

Prefer drafts, previews, staging, soft deletion, version history, rollback, and
approval before irreversible execution. When an action cannot be reversed,
Nina should communicate that before execution where practical.

## 23. Privacy and data ownership

Customer data remains customer data. NinaOS must enforce tenant isolation,
access control, retention, deletion, export, provider boundaries,
sensitive-data handling, appropriate encryption, audit access, consent, and
regional requirements. Data must not silently become shared training data.
External processing follows actual contracts and customer policy.

## 24. Failure must be visible

Provider outages, partial completion, blockers, missing permissions, stale
data, unavailable systems, uncertainty, robot faults, and channel delivery
failure must not be concealed. Nina provides a useful explanation and safest
next step.

## 25. Cost governance

NinaOS must support budgets, quotas, usage visibility, provider and worker cost
policies, approval thresholds, cost-aware routing, customer limits, alerts, and
runaway-execution prevention. Routing balances quality, risk, speed, and cost.

## 26. Digital and physical workforce

A worker may be AI software, human, workflow, service, machine, industrial
device, mobile robot, humanoid, or a mixed team. Identity, assignment,
capability, permission, task, status, telemetry, escalation, result, and audit
apply where suitable. Physical systems require additional safety boundaries.

## 27. Humanoid and robot orchestration

Future NinaOS may coordinate objectives, assignments, schedules, resources,
fleets, policy, authorization, telemetry, maintenance, exceptions, and results
for large robot fleets. It must not replace certified low-level safety.
Balance, actuators, collision avoidance, emergency stop, real-time motion, and
local interlocks remain the responsibility of local controllers. NinaOS sets
authorized work and policy; local systems control safe physical execution.
There is no current physical-control implementation at V6 adoption.

## 28. Global scale through hierarchy

Scale should use:

`Global Platform -> Organization -> Workspace/Business Unit -> Site/Region -> AI Manager/Fleet Coordinator -> Team/Fleet -> Individual`

The hierarchy must preserve tenant isolation, regional control, resilience,
and local safety.

## 29. No single global failure point

Large workforces require distributed execution, bounded local autonomy,
durable queues, idempotency, retries, leases, heartbeats, partition tolerance,
regional boundaries, graceful degradation, safe shutdown, and audit
reconciliation. Global orchestration must not eliminate local operational
independence or safety.

## 30. Modern and simple customer experience

The default experience should calmly explain what is happening, completed,
blocked, valuable, risky, awaiting a decision, and possible next. Advanced
controls may exist, but complexity is disclosed progressively.

## 31. Nina Command Center

The command center should tell one coherent operational story across priorities,
workers, work, approvals, blockers, communications, alerts, provider/channel/
device status, outcomes, quality, cost, risk, and health. It must not become a
collection of unrelated dashboards.

## 32. Customer outcomes

Success is measured by completed work, time saved, response improvement,
revenue supported, cost reduced, quality improved, errors prevented, risks
surfaced, satisfaction, continuity, and reduced human workload—not message
count alone.

## 33. Architecture before convenience

Nina identity, provider, Ready Worker definition, customer worker instance,
role, capability, permission, channel, work object, knowledge, memory,
credential, customer data, task, device, robot controller, billing, and audit
remain separate concepts. Shortcuts may not collapse these boundaries.

## 34. Authoritative architectural sequence

The immediate order remains:

1. Platform Core V1
2. RolePack System V1
3. Ready Worker Catalog V1
4. Agent Assignment V1
5. Knowledge Vault V1
6. Universal Work Objects V1
7. Channel Layer V1
8. Billing V1
9. Nina Exchange V1
10. Mobile and extended interfaces

Strategic architecture also includes the AI Provider Hub, Model Routing and
Evaluation, Nina Trust Layer, Voice, connectors, human approval, devices and
robots, fleet orchestration, and Global Control Plane. Naming future layers
does not reorder immediate implementation. Only a documented architecture
decision may do so.

## 35. Current completed foundation

The documented completed layers are Platform Core V1, RolePack System V1,
Ready Worker Catalog V1, and Agent Assignment V1. Agent Assignment provides
tenant-scoped worker instance management and lifecycle only; it does not
execute autonomous work, create separate memory, or provision channels.
Knowledge Vault V1 is the next planned architecture layer.

## 36. Documentation truth

Documentation must distinguish vision, planned architecture, implementation,
tests, deployment, experiments, and deprecation. Vision is not software;
prototype is not production; local commit is not deployment.

## 37. Zero-downtime and durability principles

PostgreSQL is authoritative hosted persistence. Hosted behavior fails closed.
Managed migrations use additive EXPAND-first changes, safe rolling deployment,
restart persistence, separated liveness/readiness, request gating, deployment
compatibility, tenant isolation, and backward compatibility. Destructive
schema change requires an explicit later CONTRACT phase. Telegram/Core and Web
runtimes remain separate; a sprint must not silently couple or break them.

## 38. Security by architecture

Security belongs in identity, authorization, secrets, adapters, channels,
knowledge, tools, execution, billing, devices, robots, and audit. Secrets must
never be committed or exposed in user-visible logs. Provider credentials are
isolated by environment and tenant policy as required.

## 39. Global usefulness

NinaOS should support multiple languages, time zones, jurisdictions, company
sizes, individuals, enterprises, public institutions, and future device and
robot ecosystems without abandoning early customers or requiring a rewrite.

## 40. Continuous adaptation

Provider, model, channel, regulatory, customer, robotics, and hardware change
must be absorbed through versioned contracts, capabilities, adapters, policy,
and migrations—not uncontrolled one-off patches.

## 41. Delivery and workflow discipline

Preserve stable checkpoints, repository-backed handoffs, full artifact delivery
when requested, evidence-based diagnosis, and focused changes. GitHub is the
code and decision record. Railway topology and deployment claims must reflect
evidence. Tests do not prove production success. No developer may claim an
untested, uncommitted, undeployed, or unverified state as something else.

## 42. Final product promise

> NinaOS unifies the world's leading artificial intelligence systems, ready AI
> workers, business tools, communication channels, people, and future robotic
> workforces in one trusted operating platform.

> The customer explains the desired outcome. Nina plans, coordinates, executes,
> checks, and reports the work through the best permitted combination of
> intelligence, tools, and workers.

Short form:

> Tell Nina the result. Nina organizes everything else.
